The Dry Eye Center at New Path Vision

Dry eye treatment starts by finding out why your eyes are dry.

Burning, gritty, watery or fluctuating vision can come from different causes. Our evaluation identifies what's contributing to your symptoms before we talk about treatment.

Schedule a Dry Eye Evaluation Call the office

Which of these sounds like you?

Burning or stinging

A persistent irritated feeling, especially by evening.

Watery eyes

Sounds backwards, but it's a common dry eye symptom.

Gritty or sandy feeling

Like there's something in your eye that isn't there.

Fluctuating vision

Vision that seems to blur and then clear as you blink.

Why dry eye is not one problem

Oil

Meibomian glands along your eyelids keep tears from evaporating too fast.

Water

Your tear glands need to produce enough tear volume in the first place.

Ocular surface

Inflammation on the eye's surface can both cause and worsen symptoms.

How we evaluate it

1Symptoms

We start with what you're feeling — burning, grittiness, watering, or vision that fluctuates through the day.

2Tear film

We check how stable your tear layer is and how quickly it breaks down between blinks.

3Oil glands

We look at your meibomian glands — the oil glands along your eyelids that keep tears from evaporating too fast.

4Inflammation & ocular surface

We check the surface of your eye for inflammation, which both causes and results from chronic dry eye.

5Personalized plan

Your treatment plan is built around what we actually find — not a one-size-fits-all bottle of drops.

Treatment options

OptiLight IPL

Light-based treatment targeting inflammation and gland function.

Warm compress / gland expression

Helps unclog and restart blocked oil glands.

Prescription drops

Medicated drops that address inflammation directly.

Punctal plugs

Small inserts that help your own tears stay on the eye longer.

See how treatments are grouped by purpose

OptiLight IPL, explained

OptiLight is a light-based treatment that can help with the inflammation and gland dysfunction behind chronic dry eye. It isn't the only option, and it isn't right for everyone — whether it's part of your plan depends on what your evaluation actually finds.
